What this is
Napster Interactive Museum

NIM is a sandbox for the original Napster client. It simulates search and downloading, but using your own local files. Only enough of the server is built to talk to the original Napster client and relive the nostalgia of the early 2000s. Built using a Python library designed around the OpenNap protocol.

Features

Feature What it does
The exhibit napster.com as it was in March 2000, fetched from the Internet Archive and handed to this client unaltered.
Chat Channels, public and private messages. Can be bridged to a live IRC channel, in which case the people answering are real.
Formats FLAC, Opus and AAC are converted to MP3 on the way in, so the client can play what it finds.
The library nim's own documentation, built from its source and readable in here without a network.


Napster was launched in June 1999 by Shawn Fanning and Sean Parker. The name came from Fanning's high school nickname, "Nappy".

Peer-to-peer file sharing already existed on IRC, Hotline and Usenet. What Napster added was focus and ease: it did one format, MP3, behind an interface anyone could use, and it surfaced music that was otherwise hard to find. Older songs. Unreleased recordings. Bootlegs.

It spread through university networks fast enough to break them. At some institutions MP3 transfers reached 61% of all external traffic, and colleges began blocking it for bandwidth reasons before anyone raised the question of liability.

In December 1999 A&M Records and others, through the RIAA, sued for contributory and vicarious copyright infringement. In April 2000 Metallica filed separately, naming some 335,000 users it said were sharing its songs. Napster was shut down in July 2001 and filed for bankruptcy in June 2002. The name was sold, and has been sold several times since, to companies with nothing to do with this software.

The March 2000 exhibit is the site nine months in, before any of that reached it, when it was still just a wordmark and a download link.
